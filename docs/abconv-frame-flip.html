abconv crossing frame: what was wrong, PR #38 and PR #42

abconv turns a head-on collision into one with the EIC crossing angle. In v0.2.1 the beams came out right, but two kinds of positions did not follow them: decay vertices stayed where the head-on event had them, and the interaction point was built for a crossing angle of the opposite sign. PR #38 repairs the decay vertices. PR #42 builds on it and repairs the interaction point, the presets and the beam energies.

Finding F2 of afterburner-workbench (dis-autoresearch F9). Measured numbers: 10x100 high divergence, NC DIS 10 < Q² < 100 GeV², events, written by docs/make_figure_data.py.

1. Words used on this page

beam, bunch
The particles do not come as a steady stream. They come in packets called bunches, about 1 cm (electrons) to several cm (protons) long and a fraction of a millimetre wide.
interaction point, IP
The place where one proton and one electron of two crossing bunches collide. It is different in every event. It is also called the primary vertex.
mrad
Milliradian. 25 mrad = 0.025 rad = 1.43°. For angles this small, sin θ ≈ tan θ ≈ θ.
crossing angle θ
The two beams do not meet head-on. At IP6 they cross at 25 mrad, so that each beam can leave through its own magnets.
head-on event
What the event generator writes: proton exactly along +z, electron exactly along −z.
afterburner
A program (here abconv) that takes head-on events and moves every particle so that the beams cross at the real angle, with the real bunch sizes.
displaced vertex
A particle such as a Λ flies some distance and then decays. The decay point is a vertex away from the IP.

2. The ePIC frame: electrons along −z, protons out at −25 mrad

The electron beam runs along −z. The proton beam leaves at θ = −25 mrad, toward −x, and +0.10 mrad vertically. x points to the centre of the accelerator ring and y points up.

Angles are drawn much larger than they are: 25 mrad about 15 times, 0.10 mrad about 1400 times.

3. The presets of v0.2.1 point the proton to +25 mrad

abconv describes each beam by a direction: an arrow of length 1. Its three numbers say how far the arrow goes along x, y and z.

\[ \hat d_e = (0,\,0,\,-1), \qquad \hat d_p = R_x(\theta_v)\,R_y(\theta_h)\,\hat z = (\sin\theta_h,\; -\cos\theta_h \sin\theta_v,\; \cos\theta_h\cos\theta_v). \]
\(\hat d_e\)
direction of the electron beam: straight along −z.
\(\hat d_p\)
direction of the proton beam: start from \(\hat z\) = (0, 0, 1), turn it about the y axis by θh (this is Ry, it moves the arrow toward x), then about the x axis by θv (Rx, it moves the arrow in y).
θh
the preset crossing_angle_hor, the horizontal crossing angle.
θv
the preset crossing_angle_ver, the vertical crossing angle.

The values in v0.2.1 and what comes out:

  • Every IP6 preset (40 of them) sets θh = +25e-3 and θv = +100e-6.
  • With these, \(\hat d_p\) ≈ (+0.025, −0.0001, 1): the configured proton points to +25 mrad horizontally, toward +x.
  • Vertically it points to −0.10 mrad, down, because rotateX(+θ) tilts +z toward −y.
  • The real machine and ePIC have −25 mrad and +0.10 mrad: both signs are opposite to the preset.
  • The protons that abconv v0.2.1 writes leave at −25.0 mrad and +0.10 mrad: right, and opposite to its own preset. Sections 6 and 7 show how.

4. Two bunches, one collision: p, e, ct and z

Each collision comes from a proton at position p in its bunch and an electron at position e in its bunch. Both abconv and eicBeamShape set

\[ ct = \frac{e - p}{2}, \qquad z = \frac{e + p}{2} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad e = z + ct, \quad p = z - ct . \]
t = 0
the moment the centres of the two bunches pass through each other. That point is z = 0.
p
where the colliding proton is at t = 0, measured along z from the centre of its bunch. The protons move toward +z, so p > 0 is the front of the proton bunch.
e
where the colliding electron is at t = 0, measured along z from the centre of its bunch. The electrons move toward −z, so e > 0 is the back of the electron bunch.
ct
the time of the collision, multiplied by the speed of light c. It is then a length (mm), the distance light travels in the time t. Both beams move at almost exactly c.
z
the place of the collision along the beam line.

Where the formulas come from: the proton is at p + ct, the electron at e − ct. They collide when they are at the same place: p + ct = e − ct. Solve for ct, then put it back to get z.

5. The collision point moves toward −x as z + ct grows

With a crossing angle the bunches would pass through each other at a slant and overlap badly. Crab cavities therefore tilt both bunches so that, at the IP, both lie along the line halfway between the two beams (the bisector). For a proton beam at −25 mrad that line is at −θ/2 = −12.5 mrad.

With e = z + ct from section 4, the right answer for ePIC is

\[ x_{\rm IP} = -\frac{\theta}{2}\,(z + ct) + \xi_x, \qquad \theta = 25\ \text{mrad}, \]

where ξx is the random position across the bunch width (about 0.1 mm). The slope of IP x against z + ct must be −12.5 mrad. This slope is the number that tells right from wrong on this page.

6. A boost shows the event to a moving observer

A boost answers the question: how does the same event look to an observer who moves at constant velocity? It is the Lorentz transformation between two frames that move against each other, with no turning involved. The boost vector \(\vec\beta\) is the velocity of that motion divided by c: its direction is the direction of the motion, its length the speed as a fraction of the speed of light. |β| = 0.0125 means 1.25 % of c.

An everyday picture: rain falls straight down. Seen from a car that drives forward, the rain comes slanted from the front. Nothing happened to the rain. The slant is the car's velocity, seen from the car, and it points backward: opposite to the car's motion.

That last point gives two ways to use one vector, and they differ by a sign:

For particles that move at nearly c, a sideways boost of size β tilts the direction by an angle of about β radians. So a boost of 0.0125 across the beams tilts both beams by 12.5 mrad, to the same side.

The boost vector abconv uses

It is half the sum of the two beam directions (Afterburner.cc v0.2.1, lines 323–328):

\[ \vec\beta = \tfrac12(\hat d_p + \hat d_e) \approx \Big(\tfrac{\theta_h}{2},\; -\tfrac{\theta_v}{2},\; -\tfrac{\theta_h^2}{4}\Big). \]

Why half the sum: a boost of θ/2 across the beams tilts both beams by θ/2. A rotation by θ/2 then brings the electron back onto −z and takes the proton to the full θ. Boost and rotation together make the crossing angle.

7. In v0.2.1 two flipped signs cancel in the momenta

abconv calls evt.boost(β) (Converter.cc line 84). This is the passive kind: the particles receive Λ(−β). With the preset θh = +25 mrad, β points to +x, so the momenta are pushed by 12.5 mrad toward −x: the first flip. The rotation that follows is built to match it (the line carries the comment // TODO back to -acos?) and also turns toward −x: the second flip.

Step by step: what v0.2.1 does against what is needed

"Needed" is the straightforward way: preset −25 mrad, active boost, rotation toward the proton side.

Three of the four pieces are identical. That is why directions, divergences and crab kicks of v0.2.1 are right.

The fourth piece changes the beam energies

Two effects add up for each beam:

  • The sideways push adds a little speed to every particle. Both beams gain θ²/8 = 0.78 × 10⁻⁴ of their energy.
  • The push along +z goes with the proton (it flies toward +z) and against the electron. The proton gains θ²/4 = 1.56 × 10⁻⁴, the electron loses the same.

8. Positions were not moved with the momenta

Decay vertices stayed in the head-on frame (repaired by PR #38)

GenEvent::boost and abconv's rotation loop act on momenta only. Vertex positions stayed as in the head-on event. A Λ that flies forward got its momentum turned by 25 mrad, while the point where it decays stayed on the old line.

A position has a time as well, and a boost mixes the two. So the input must carry correct vertex times: with all times set to 0, half of the error stays (12.6 mrad).

The interaction point is built from the preset: +25 mrad instead of −25 mrad (repaired by PR #42)

The IP is made by generate_vertx_with_bunch_interaction(), which is line for line eicBeamShape's. It works in two steps and takes the angle θh straight from the preset.

\[ x' = \sin\tfrac{\theta_h}{2}\; ct,\quad z' = \tfrac{e+p}{2} \quad\xrightarrow{\;\text{turn by }\theta_h/2\;}\quad x = x'\cos\tfrac{\theta_h}{2} + z'\sin\tfrac{\theta_h}{2} \;\approx\; \frac{\theta_h}{2}\,(z + ct) + \xi_x . \]

ξx is the random position across the bunch width. eicBeamShape runs this with −0.025 and gets the −12.5 mrad of section 5. abconv v0.2.1 runs it with +25e-3 and gets +12.5 mrad: the drawing above is the mirror image, in x, of what the machine does.

Why the momenta are right and the IP is not: the momenta went through the two flipped signs of section 7, which cancel. The IP goes through none of them. shift_position_to() places it exactly as computed here. PR #38 does not change it.

9. Measured: the IP slope has the wrong sign before, the right sign after

All abconv rows ran on the same head-on Pythia8 file and share abconv's random numbers (the seed is fixed, F1), so their IP slopes share their noise. Pythia8's row has its own random numbers. The "signs fixed" row is the direct test of 2026-11-07 (v0.2.1 with evt.boost(−β), −acos and presets −25e-3 / −100e-6). PR #42 reports the same IP slope, −12.29 ± 0.10 mrad.

Negating the presets alone is no fix: the beams move to +25 mrad and the crab kicks flip, so the IP is again opposite to the beams.

10. PR #42: one transform, presets with the ePIC angles

  • One transform. Afterburner::crossing_transform builds a rotation of +z onto the head-on beam axis, then an active boost perpendicular to that axis (second row of the figure in section 7). No passive boost, no acos with a chosen sign.
  • Momenta and vertices alike. Converter.cc applies boost * rotation to every momentum and to every vertex four-position. PR #38's separate vertex loop is absorbed.
  • Presets mean what they say. crossing_angle_hor is the hadron beam's angle to +z in the x–z plane, positive toward +x: −25e-3 at IP6 (40 presets), +35e-3 at IP8 (14 presets, before −35e-3). crossing_angle_ver is its angle in the y–z plane, positive toward +y: it stays +100e-6, and now means up.
  • The vertex code is not touched. It reads −25e-3 and gives −12.5 mrad, as eicBeamShape does.
  • Beam energies. The boost is perpendicular to both beams, so both energies grow by the same factor γ ≈ 1 + θ²/8 = 1 + 7.8 × 10⁻⁵. For nominal energies at the crossing, generate head-on at E·cos(θ/2).
  • Massive beams. The boost is computed from the velocities |p|/E of the input beams, so a 100 GeV proton leaves exactly along its direction.
  • README. A coordinate-system section with the frame of section 2 and its sources, and the input requirements.

Two things to know when PR #42 is merged: the output changes for a given random seed, and code that sets crossing_angle_hor itself must change the sign.

How large is the IP error? 12.5 mrad × 7 mm ≈ 0.09 mm at 1σ of the electron bunch, against a luminous width of 0.1 mm. Nothing in the kinematics depends on it. It matters for anything that uses the shape of the luminous region or the link between IP x and time, such as a collision time taken from the vertex.

11. The benchmark's balance check failed on a correct transform, and now compares event by event

Energy and momentum are conserved: in every event the four numbers (px, py, pz, E) of all outgoing particles, added up, must equal those of the two beams. The difference is the imbalance. The benchmark script closure.py checks that abconv does not make it worse. With PR #42 this check failed in 7 setup-runs, although the transform was right.

The cause: Pythia8 itself writes a few events that miss balance

  • In setup s2, 10x100_hiacc, it is event 9476. Its imbalance from Pythia8 is (0, 0, −1.34e-5, +1.34e-5)·√s.
  • A Lorentz transform changes the components of any four-vector that is not zero. abconv scales this one by exactly 1 + θ²/8, the same factor as the beam energies in section 7.
  • The growth is 1.34e-5 × 7.8e-5 ≈ 1.0e-9 of √s, right at the old absolute allowance of 1e-9.

The fix in closure.py

  • The four-momentum balance is now compared event by event.
  • In each event the output's imbalance may exceed the input's by 10 % of it, plus the old absolute 1e-9 (BALANCE_GROWTH = 0.1).
  • The rotation and the boost of about half the crossing angle raise any component by a few percent at most. Pythia8 inputs miss balance by at most 3.8e-5 of √s, so the extra allowance stays below 4e-6 of √s. A wrong transformation shows at about 1e-4.
  • Recorded as decision D-default-10.

Checks of the new rule

  • Same failures reproduced. The 7 failing setup-runs were rerun at 20 000 events with the same seeds. The old check gave the identical failures, with input imbalances matching the saved ones to 7 digits.
  • New check passes. All 7 now agree, with an excess of at most 2e-15.
  • Controls still fail. Beams left untransformed: excess 4.1e-2. One final-state particle per event scaled by 1 − 1e-6: excess 1.4e-6.
